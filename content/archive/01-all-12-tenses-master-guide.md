# The Complete Master Guide to All 12 English Tenses

In English, every tense is a combination of **Time** (*Present, Past, Future*) and **Aspect** (*Simple, Continuous, Perfect, Perfect Continuous*). 

Together, these form the **12 core tenses**. This guide covers the precise formulas, practical usage, signal markers, and high-level nuances for all 12 tenses.

---

## 🧭 The 12 Tenses Overview Matrix

| Aspect | Present | Past | Future |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Simple** | `S + V1 (s/es)` | `S + V2` | `S + will + V1` |
| **Continuous** | `S + am/is/are + V-ing` | `S + was/were + V-ing` | `S + will be + V-ing` |
| **Perfect** | `S + have/has + V3` | `S + had + V3` | `S + will have + V3` |
| **Perfect Continuous**| `S + have/has been + V-ing` | `S + had been + V-ing` | `S + will have been + V-ing` |

## 1. The Present Tenses

### 1.1 Simple Present
* **Formula:**  
  * Affirmative: `Subject + V1 (s/es)`  
  * Negative: `Subject + do/does not + V1`  
  * Question: `Do/Does + Subject + V1?`
* **When to use:**
  1. **Habits & Routines:** Actions that happen regularly (*"He reviews financial reports every morning."*)
  2. **General Truths & Scientific Facts:** Universal realities (*"Water boils at 100°C."*)
  3. **Permanent Situations:** Long-term states (*"She works in Singapore."*)
  4. **Fixed Timetables / Schedules:** Events fixed by an official schedule (*"The conference starts at 09:00 tomorrow."*)
* **Signal Words:** *always, usually, often, seldom, rarely, every day/week, on Mondays, generally.*
* **Nuance / B2 Pitfall:**  
  * **Stative Verbs:** Verbs of perception, thought, emotion, or possession (*know, believe, understand, belong, seem, prefer*) are strictly used in the Simple Present, **not** Present Continuous (*"I understand the problem"*, never *"I am understanding"*).

---

### 1.2 Present Continuous (Progressive)
* **Formula:**  
  * Affirmative: `Subject + am/is/are + V-ing`  
  * Negative: `Subject + am/is/are + not + V-ing`  
  * Question: `Am/Is/Are + Subject + V-ing?`
* **When to use:**
  1. **Actions Happening Right Now:** At the exact moment of speaking (*"The system is running diagnostic tests."*)
  2. **Temporary Situations:** Ongoing over a period around now, but not permanent (*"We are living in a rented apartment this month."*)
  3. **Definite Future Arrangements:** Personal plans already organized (*"I am meeting the supervisor this Friday."*)
  4. **Trends and Changing Situations:** (*"Global temperatures are rising rapidly."*)
* **Signal Words:** *now, right now, at the moment, currently, these days, this semester.*
* **Nuance / B2 Pitfall:**  
  * When used with *always* (*"He is always interrupting people"*), it expresses annoyance or mild criticism rather than a mere routine.

---

### 1.3 Present Perfect
* **Formula:**  
  * Affirmative: `Subject + have/has + V3 (Past Participle)`  
  * Negative: `Subject + have/has not + V3`  
  * Question: `Have/Has + Subject + V3?`
* **When to use:**
  1. **Life Experience (Time Unspecified):** (*"She has visited seven countries."*)
  2. **Action Completed with Present Relevance/Result:** (*"I have lost my passport [so I cannot board now].*)
  3. **Unfinished Time Periods:** (*"We have published three papers this year [the year is still ongoing].*)
  4. **Actions Continuing from Past to Present (with stative verbs):** (*"They have known each other since 2018."*)
* **Signal Words:** *already, yet, just, ever, never, since, for, recently, so far, to date.*
* **Nuance / B2 Pitfall:**  
  * Never use Present Perfect with a specific, finished past time marker.  
    * ❌ *"I have sent the email yesterday."*  
    * ✔️ *"I sent the email yesterday."* (Past Simple)  
    * ✔️ *"I have sent the email."* (Present Perfect)

---

### 1.4 Present Perfect Continuous
* **Formula:**  
  * Affirmative: `Subject + have/has been + V-ing`  
  * Negative: `Subject + have/has not been + V-ing`  
  * Question: `Have/Has + Subject + been + V-ing?`
* **When to use:**
  1. **Actions Starting in the Past and Continuing Right Now:** Emphasizing duration (*"I have been working on this manuscript for six hours."*)
  2. **Recent Action with Observable Present Evidence:** (*"The ground is wet; it has been raining."*)
* **Signal Words:** *for, since, all day, lately, recently, how long.*
* **Nuance Comparison (Present Perfect vs. Present Perfect Continuous):**
  * *Present Perfect* focuses on the **result/completion**: *"I have written the report"* (it is finished).
  * *Present Perfect Continuous* focuses on the **process/duration**: *"I have been writing the report"* (may or may not be finished).

## 2. The Past Tenses

### 2.1 Simple Past
* **Formula:**  
  * Affirmative: `Subject + V2`  
  * Negative: `Subject + did not + V1`  
  * Question: `Did + Subject + V1?`
* **When to use:**
  1. **Completed Action in the Past:** Finished at a specific time (*"The experiment concluded last night."*)
  2. **Sequence of Past Events:** Narrative order (*"She entered the room, opened the folder, and began her presentation."*)
  3. **Past Habits (now discontinued):** (*"He lived in London when he was younger."*)
* **Signal Words:** *yesterday, ago, in 2020, last week/month, when I was young.*
* **Nuance / B2 Pitfall:**  
  * Remember that in negative and question forms, the verb returns to **V1** because *did/didn't* already carries the past tense (*"Did you see the result?"*, never *"Did you saw..."*).

---

### 2.2 Past Continuous
* **Formula:**  
  * Affirmative: `Subject + was/were + V-ing`  
  * Negative: `Subject + was/were not + V-ing`  
  * Question: `Was/Were + Subject + V-ing?`
* **When to use:**
  1. **Interrupted Past Action:** Ongoing action interrupted by another (*"We were calibrating the machine when the power failed."*)
  2. **Parallel Past Actions:** Two actions happening simultaneously in the past (*"While John was writing the code, Sarah was conducting user interviews."*)
  3. **Background Atmosphere:** Setting the scene in writing (*"The sun was setting and the wind was howling as they arrived."*)
* **Signal Words:** *while, as, when, at that exact moment.*
* **Rule:** Use *while / as* before the continuous clause, and *when* before the interrupting simple past clause.

---

### 2.3 Past Perfect
* **Formula:**  
  * Affirmative: `Subject + had + V3`  
  * Negative: `Subject + had not + V3`  
  * Question: `Had + Subject + V3?`
* **When to use:**
  1. **The "Past Before the Past":** An action completed before another past event (*"By the time the ambulance arrived, the patient had recovered."*)
  2. **Conditionals (Type 3):** Unreal past conditions (*"If they had tested the software, the bug would not have occurred."*)
* **Signal Words:** *before, after, by the time, already, until that day, had never.*
* **Nuance / B2 Pitfall:**  
  * Don't use Past Perfect just because something happened a long time ago. It is **only** required when you need to make the chronological order between **two past events** explicit.

---

### 2.4 Past Perfect Continuous
* **Formula:**  
  * Affirmative: `Subject + had been + V-ing`  
  * Negative: `Subject + had not been + V-ing`  
  * Question: `Had + Subject + been + V-ing?`
* **When to use:**
  1. **Duration of an Ongoing Action Before Another Past Event:** (*"They had been searching for the source code for three days before they finally found it."*)
  2. **Past Action with a Visible Result in the Past:** (*"He was exhausted because he had been driving all night."*)
* **Signal Words:** *for, since, before, by the time.*

---

## 3. The Future Tenses

### 3.1 Simple Future
* **Formula:**  
  * Form A (Will): `Subject + will + V1`  
  * Form B (Be going to): `Subject + am/is/are + going to + V1`
* **When to use:**
  1. **Spontaneous Decisions (Will):** Decided at the moment of speech (*"The phone is ringing. I'll get it."*)
  2. **Predictions Based on Belief / Opinion (Will):** (*"Experts believe automation will reshape the economy."*)
  3. **Prior Intentions / Plans (Be going to):** Decided before speaking (*"We are going to launch the prototype next month."*)
  4. **Predictions Based on Present Evidence (Be going to):** (*"Look at those dark clouds; it is going to rain."*)
* **Signal Words:** *tomorrow, next week, soon, in 2030, probably, definitely.*

---

### 3.2 Future Continuous
* **Formula:**  
  * Affirmative: `Subject + will be + V-ing`  
  * Negative: `Subject + will not be + V-ing`  
  * Question: `Will + Subject + be + V-ing?`
* **When to use:**
  1. **Action in Progress at a Specific Future Moment:** (*"This time next week, I will be presenting our research at the symposium."*)
  2. **Routine or Inevitable Future Events:** (*"I will be passing by the library anyway, so I can return your book."*)
  3. **Polite Inquiries About Plans:** (*"Will you be joining us for lunch today?"*)
* **Signal Words:** *at this time tomorrow, this time next year, in an hour.*

---

### 3.3 Future Perfect
* **Formula:**  
  * Affirmative: `Subject + will have + V3`  
  * Negative: `Subject + will not have + V3`  
  * Question: `Will + Subject + have + V3?`
* **When to use:**
  1. **Action That Will Be Completed Before a Specific Point in the Future:** (*"By December 2027, the team will have finalized the clinical trials."*)
* **Signal Words:** *by [time], by the time, before, by then, in two years' time.*
* **Nuance / B2 Pitfall:**  
  * In the time clause after *by the time*, always use **Simple Present**, never *will*:  
    * ❌ *"By the time you will arrive, we will have left."*  
    * ✔️ *"By the time you **arrive**, we **will have left**."*

---

### 3.4 Future Perfect Continuous
* **Formula:**  
  * Affirmative: `Subject + will have been + V-ing`  
  * Negative: `Subject + will not have been + V-ing`  
  * Question: `Will + Subject + have been + V-ing?`
* **When to use:**
  1. **Duration of an Ongoing Activity Projected into a Future Point:** (*"By next month, she will have been teaching at this university for 10 years."*)
* **Signal Words:** *for [duration] by [future time], by the time.*

## 4. Master Comparative Cheat Sheet

| Tense | Key Auxiliary | Example | Primary Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Simple Present** | V1 / does / do | *The sun rises.* | Fact, routine, permanent truth |
| **Present Continuous** | am / is / are | *She is writing.* | Action in progress right now |
| **Present Perfect** | have / has | *They have finished.* | Past action connected to present |
| **Present Perfect Cont.**| have / has been | *I have been studying.* | Action continuing up to now |
| **Simple Past** | V2 / did | *We completed it.* | Finished action at specific past time |
| **Past Continuous** | was / were | *He was sleeping.* | Ongoing past action interrupted |
| **Past Perfect** | had | *She had left.* | Event completed before another past event |
| **Past Perfect Cont.** | had been | *They had been waiting.* | Duration up to a past reference point |
| **Simple Future** | will / going to | *We will notify you.* | Spontaneous decision or prediction |
| **Future Continuous** | will be | *I will be traveling.* | Action ongoing at future point |
| **Future Perfect** | will have | *He will have graduated.* | Finished before a future deadline |
| **Future Perfect Cont.** | will have been | *We will have been living.*| Duration measured at a future point |
