# The Tricky `'d` Contraction: Had vs. Would vs. Did

In written and spoken English, the suffix **`'d`** attached to pronouns or question words is one of the most compressed contractions in the language. Depending on the grammatical context, it represents **`WOULD`**, **`HAD`**, or **`DID`**.

---

## 1. Attached to Pronouns (*I'd, you'd, he'd, she'd, we'd, they'd*)

When `'d` is appended to a personal pronoun, it can **only** mean `WOULD` or `HAD`. The decisive clue is the verb form immediately following it:

### A. `'d` = WOULD (Followed by Base Form / Verb 1)
Used for conditional statements, hypothetical scenarios, polite requests, and preferences:

* **Preferences (`would rather`):**  
  * *I'd rather analyze the raw data first.*  
    $\rightarrow$ *I **would** rather analyze...*
* **Polite requests / Desires (`would like`):**  
  * *We'd appreciate your prompt feedback on the draft.*  
    $\rightarrow$ *We **would** appreciate...*
* **Conditionals (Type 2 - Hypothetical):**  
  * *If resources were unlimited, we'd expand the study.*  
    $\rightarrow$ *...we **would** expand...*

### B. `'d` = HAD (Followed by Past Participle / Verb 3)
Used for the Past Perfect tense (an action completed prior to another past reference point) and the idiomatic warning `had better`:

* **Past Perfect (`had + V3`):**  
  * *By the time the peer review concluded, the team'd revised the methodology.*  
    $\rightarrow$ *...the team **had** revised...*
* **Recommendations / Advisories (`had better` + V1):**  
  * *You'd better verify the citation before publishing.*  
    $\rightarrow$ *You **had** better verify...*  
    *(Note: Although followed by a base verb, the fixed expression is always 'had better', never 'would better'.)*

---

## 2. Attached to Question Words (*Where'd, Why'd, How'd, What'd*)

In informal correspondence, interviews, and spoken English, `'d` attached to interrogative pronouns represents **`DID`**:

| Contraction | Uncontracted Form | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Where'd** | *Where did* | *Where'd they obtain that dataset?* |
| **Why'd** | *Why did* | *Why'd the committee reject the initial proposal?* |
| **How'd** | *How did* | *How'd the authors reach this conclusion?* |
| **What'd** | *What did* | *What'd the supervisor recommend?* |

> [!NOTE]  
> `did` is **never** contracted onto personal pronouns in affirmative statements (e.g., you cannot say *"I'd the experiment yesterday"* for *"I did the experiment"*).

---

## 3. Quick Reference Matrix

| Pattern | Represents | Example Sentence | Meaning / Equivalent |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `'d` + **Verb 1 (Base)** | **WOULD** | *They'd support the initiative.* | *They would support...* |
| `'d` + **Verb 3 (Past Part.)** | **HAD** | *They'd supported the initiative earlier.* | *They had supported...* |
| `'d rather` | **WOULD** | *I'd rather re-evaluate the hypothesis.* | *I would rather...* |
| `'d better` | **HAD** | *We'd better document the discrepancy.* | *We had better...* |
| `Wh-` + `'d` | **DID** | *How'd you resolve the sampling bias?* | *How did you resolve...* |

---

## 4. The Dual `'d` Benchmark (Conditional Type 3)

Observe how both forms appear within a single academic conditional sentence:

> *"If the researchers **'d documented** the anomalies earlier, they **'d have prevented** the retraction."*

* **...researchers 'd documented** $\rightarrow$ Followed by *documented* (V3) = **had documented**
* **...they 'd have** $\rightarrow$ Followed by *have* (V1) = **would have**
